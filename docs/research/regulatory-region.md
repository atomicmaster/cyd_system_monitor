# Regulatory Region: legal gate or channel-plan preference?

Research date: 2026-09-15

This note asks whether the Monitor Device's first-start "Regulatory Region" setting (CONTEXT.md: "The Operator-confirmed country or region that determines which radio discovery behavior and WiFi channels the Monitor Device may use") needs to be framed as a legal-compliance gate, or whether it is really a WiFi channel-plan preference with no compliance content. Statements under **Verified** cite regulator texts, ESP-IDF's own header/API documentation, and the reviewed projects' own docs/source. The planning review corrects the original legal conclusion in §1 and the final recommendation; the channel API and precedent discussion remains background to be checked against the pinned implementation dependencies. Statements under **Recommendation** are project design judgment. This note takes ADR 0001 (observational-only radio monitoring: no transmission, no association, no active BLE scanning, no interference) as a fixed constraint and does not revisit it.

## 1. Correction: equipment rules and the channel-setting decision

**Corrected during planning review.** The initial conclusion that the reviewed frameworks regulate transmitters but not receivers was too broad. It must not be used to describe passive reception as universally unregulated.

**Verified.** US Part 15 explicitly includes receiver categories in § 15.101(a). Section 15.101(b) limits which receivers require those authorizations and provides an exemption for most receivers above 960 MHz, while retaining § 15.5 obligations. That is a specific provision, not a blanket statement that receivers or the complete digital appliance are outside regulation. [47 CFR § 15.101](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/15.101)

**Verified.** EU RED Article 2 includes products that intentionally receive radio waves. Article 1 covers making equipment available on the market and putting it into service; Article 3 specifies essential requirements. An application-level channel preset does not establish conformity with those requirements. [Directive 2014/53/EU, Articles 1–3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32014L0053)

The original Japan discussion cited Article 2's receive-only exclusion from the definition of a radio station and Article 4's station-licensing requirement. That narrower licensing distinction cannot establish a universal exemption from equipment, interception, privacy, or local-law requirements. [MIC/Soumu, Radio Law translation](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/joho_tsusin/eng/Resources/laws/radiolaw2003/RL-index.html)

**Recommendation.** Keep the channel setting as a scheduling preference. This is a product-design decision about what the control actually changes, not a legal conclusion that reception is unrestricted or that selecting a region proves compliance.

## 2. ESP-IDF's country-code API: what it actually gates

**Verified.** ESP-IDF's `wifi_country_t` struct is documented as "Structure describing Wi-Fi country-based regional restrictions," with fields `cc` (country code string), `schan`/`nchan` (start channel / total channel count of "the allowed 2.4GHz Wi-Fi channels"), `max_tx_power` — documented explicitly as "used for getting Wi-Fi maximum transmitting power; call `esp_wifi_set_max_tx_power` to set the maximum transmitting power" — and `policy`. [ESP-IDF `esp_wifi_types_generic.h`](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/components/esp_wifi/include/esp_wifi_types_generic.h) The `esp_wifi_set_country()` doc comment itself warns: "It is discouraged to call this API since this doesn't validate the per-country rules, it's up to the user to fill in all fields according to local regulations. Please use `esp_wifi_set_country_code` instead." Its other effects are about outgoing frames: "When the country info is changed... the country IE in probe response/beacon of the soft-AP is also changed," and under `WIFI_COUNTRY_POLICY_AUTO`, "the country info of the AP to which the station is connected is used" (i.e., it tracks whichever AP the device associates to). [ESP-IDF `esp_wifi.h`](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/components/esp_wifi/include/esp_wifi.h) `esp_wifi_set_promiscuous()` is documented with no reference to country/channel restriction at all — it is a plain enable/disable toggle. `esp_wifi_set_channel()`, the API promiscuous-mode sniffers use to move the radio, is documented purely in terms of STA/AP operating-mode preconditions (don't call while scanning/connecting, 5 GHz secondary-channel derivation, NVS persistence) with no stated country-code gate in its own doc comment. [ESP-IDF `esp_wifi.h`](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/components/esp_wifi/include/esp_wifi.h) The programming guide's own summary text ties country configuration to "country policy," "max transmit power," and the country information element broadcast by a soft-AP — all TX-side concerns — with no documented statement that promiscuous-mode reception is restricted to the configured country's channel range. [ESP-IDF Programming Guide, Wi-Fi API reference](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32/api-reference/network/esp_wifi.html)

In practice, `schan`/`nchan` do constrain the argument accepted by `esp_wifi_set_channel()` in station/AP-mode use (this is corroborated informally by ESP32 developer forum reports of channels 12/13 being rejected under the default "01"/US-shaped world-safe country info, and accepted once `nchan` is widened) — but this is the same single channel-set call used for STA/AP association and for promiscuous listening; there is no separate, documented RX-only allow-list independent from the configured country's channel window. The gate, where it exists, is a byproduct of one shared "which channel can this radio tune to" call, not a purpose-built passive-listening restriction, and Espressif's own documentation frames the whole feature around transmit power and outgoing country IEs.

**Recommendation.** Treat country/channel configuration as a technical scheduler input, not permission the firmware grants the Operator. Verify the exact default channel range and accepted tuning calls against the pinned ESP-IDF version during M1a/M5; this note does not establish that the world default supports channels 1–14. Silence in an API comment is not proof that a receive-side restriction cannot exist.

## 3. Channel-plan differences: a scheduling concern, not a compliance one

**Verified.** The regional variation that actually exists in 2.4 GHz WiFi is channel-plan width: `esp_wifi_set_country_code`'s own doc comment shows the worked example of US-shaped info (`schan=1, nchan=11`, i.e. channels 1–11) versus Japan-shaped info (`schan=1, nchan=14`, i.e. channels 1–14) as the two ends of the range ESP-IDF models. [ESP-IDF `esp_wifi.h`](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/components/esp_wifi/include/esp_wifi.h) This matches the well-known real-world split (channels 1–11 in the US/Canada, 1–13 in most of Europe, 1–14 in Japan, with 14 restricted to 802.11b-only equipment there) reflected in ESP-IDF's channel-count field itself.

Kismet, the reference open-source WiFi/BLE monitoring tool, documents channel selection purely as a hardware/throughput tradeoff. Its channel-hopping doc discusses hop rate, per-source channel lists, and the completeness/coverage tradeoff of hopping vs. staying locked to one channel — with no mention of "region," "country," or legal compliance anywhere on that page. [Kismet, Channels & Hopping](https://www.kismetwireless.net/docs/readme/datasources/channelhop/)

**Recommendation.** Channel selection lets the scheduler concentrate dwell time on channels relevant to the deployment. A channel not sampled remains outside observed coverage; it must not be labeled quiet, and a Coverage Gap alone is not an Alert. The setting makes no legal-compliance claim.

## 4. BLE: no regional channel-plan variation to begin with

**Verified.** The Bluetooth Core Specification, Low Energy Controller / Physical Layer Specification, defines 40 RF channels spanning 2402–2480 MHz at 2 MHz spacing, of which three (channel indices 37, 38, 39, at 2402/2426/2480 MHz) are the primary advertising channels and the remaining 37 are used after connection. [Bluetooth SIG Core Specification, Physical Layer Specification](https://www.bluetooth.com/wp-content/uploads/Files/Specification/HTML/Core-54/out/en/low-energy-controller/physical-layer-specification.html) This channel plan is fixed by the Core Specification itself, not parameterized by country the way WiFi's channel count is; no region-specific advertising-channel variant is defined. Because ADR 0001 already restricts BLE observation to passive advertisement listening with no active scan requests or transmission, and the channel set being listened to is not region-dependent in the first place, there is nothing for a "Regulatory Region" setting to actually change on the BLE side.

**Recommendation.** "Regulatory Region" should have no effect on BLE behavior at all. If the setting is kept, its scope should be documented as WiFi-only.

## 5. Precedent from existing tools

**Verified.** Kismet has no region/country compliance gate in its documented data-source configuration; channel lists are driven by what the capture interface/driver reports as supported, and its docs frame channel selection entirely as a technical capture-completeness concern (see §3). [Kismet, Channels & Hopping](https://www.kismetwireless.net/docs/readme/datasources/channelhop/) No "confirm your regulatory region before scanning" step appears in Kismet's reviewed documentation.

The WiGLE Android client (the reference wardriving app already used as this project's export-format precedent) drives WiFi/BLE scanning entirely through the Android platform's own scan APIs and has no separate operator-facing "region" confirmation step of its own in its documented data flow; regulatory-domain handling, where it exists, is delegated to the OS/driver layer beneath the app rather than surfaced as an app-level legal gate. [WiGLE Android `wigle-wifi-wardriving` repository](https://github.com/wiglenet/wigle-wifi-wardriving)

Independent ESP32 promiscuous-mode sniffer projects reviewed (e.g., simple channel-configurable WiFi packet sniffers and probe-request loggers built directly on `esp_wifi_set_promiscuous`/`esp_wifi_set_channel`) expose a channel or channel-list parameter as a plain integer/config value with no first-run "select your country" onboarding step; region, where handled at all, is a hardcoded or user-supplied channel range rather than a compliance confirmation. [e.g. ESP32 packet-sniffer PoC](https://github.com/timschmolka/esp32-sniffer)

**Recommendation.** The precedent across the class of tool this project belongs to is consistent: region is either absent entirely or handled as a silent technical default (driver/OS-level), never as an operator-facing legal-consent gate blocking first use. This project would be an outlier among its own reference tools if it kept a blocking, legally-worded confirmation step for this.

## Recommendation

Keep **WiFi Channel Plan** as an ordinary scheduling preference, scoped to WiFi with BLE unaffected. First start need not block on a region confirmation. An imported plan applies like other portable settings, without a separate legal-consent step.

The default and regional presets must name concrete channel ranges supported by the pinned driver and validated on the hardware. `World-safe` is a preset label, not a declaration of legal compliance or proof of a particular channel count. Changes trade channel coverage against revisit time; their effect should be visible.

This decision does not depend on the withdrawn claim that reception is uniformly unregulated. Product documentation should explain that the setting selects listening channels and retain the general reminder that Operators are responsible for complying with their local law. The reviewed sources do not establish the complete appliance's compliance or exhaust interception, privacy, equipment, and jurisdiction-specific requirements.
